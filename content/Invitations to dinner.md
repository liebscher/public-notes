Sometimes, my needs for socialization[^1] get out of balance. I might get overstimulated or oversocialized, (especially as a result of living in New York City, where there is *always* something to do or someone to see) or maybe isolated and lonely.

I haven't figured out the secret to maintaining homeostasis. This week I'm trying something new: not making plans in advance. Normally I find my calendar for the week fills up with plans a week or two in advance. Not every night, but usually most. In the past I've even challenged myself to do *something* out in the world every day or night of the week. But this week I'm doing the opposite.

I was feeling oversocialized, and I attributed this to the feeling of obligation to the the social plans in my calendar.

So I cut out the plans and committed to staying in, or going out, as my body and mind ask me to. It's been a breath of fresh air; much more fluid and forgiving. The city can be a lot, and this seems like a trick to managing its wonderful burden.

Last night I cooked a delicious dinner. Tonight, my pantry felt unstocked and I felt too tired to cook. I decided to treat myself to a burrito from a local spot, and on my walk over decided I actually could use a little socialization. Not an entire night's worth, but I wanted to just have a little conversation.

I called a friend in the neighborhood and asked if he wanted to sit on a park bench to have dinner with me. Or, rather, watch me eat my burrito. It was a cool, windy evening yet we had a calm and pleasant meandering conversation about work, travel, and games. Together for less than an hour, yet I walked away feeling restored and energized. Let this be a lesson to myself: invite your friends for dinner, even for just a little while.

[^1]: Could this be a parallel phenomenon to [need for cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Need_for_cognition)?
